Turning 65? Here's how to start Medicare without missing a step.

By Christopher Apana, Florida license W197973, NPN 17317513.

You get one seven-month window to sign up for Medicare on your own schedule. Miss it and you can pay a penalty for life. This guide walks through the window, the 2027 costs, your three ways to fill Medicare's gaps, and a checklist you can print. If you'd rather talk it through, call (561) 918-2999 and a licensed agent in Boca Raton will.

Your seven-month window

Your Initial Enrollment Period starts three months before the month you turn 65 and ends three months after it. Sign up in the three months before, and coverage starts the first day of your birthday month. Sign up during or after, and coverage starts the first of the next month.

What Medicare costs in 2027

Part AHospital

Most people pay no premium. Hospital deductible $1,788 (projected).

Part BDoctors and outpatient

$209.50 a month (projected), with a deductible of about $292 to $310 (projected). Higher incomes pay more.

Part DPrescriptions

Premium varies by plan. Deductible up to $700. Out-of-pocket drug costs capped at $2,400.

Part A and Part B figures are projections. CMS has not announced the final 2027 amounts, and we will update this page when it does.

Three ways to fill the gaps

  • Original Medicare with a Medigap plan and a Part D drug planAny doctor who takes Medicare, predictable costs, a separate monthly premium.
  • A Medicare Advantage planOne card, often drug coverage included, a network of doctors and a yearly out-of-pocket limit.
  • Keep employer coverage for nowIf you or your spouse still work at a company with 20 or more employees, you may be able to delay Part B without a penalty. See Working past 65.

We compare 83 Medicare Advantage plans from 12 organizations in Broward and Palm Beach and explain the trade-offs for your doctors and prescriptions.

The two penalties that last for life

10%

Part B late penalty

Added to your premium for each full 12 months you could have had Part B and didn't.

1%

Part D late penalty

Of the national base premium for each month you went without drug coverage that's at least as good as Medicare's.

Medicare for veterans: how it fits with the benefits you earned.

By Christopher Apana, Florida license W197973, NPN 17317513.

VA health care and Medicare are separate systems, and neither pays the other's bills. Many veterans in Broward and Palm Beach keep both, so they can use VA facilities and also see doctors outside the VA. This guide covers how each benefit treats Medicare and what to decide at 65.

Three benefits, three sets of rules

VA health care

You aren't required to take Medicare. But Medicare doesn't pay for care at VA facilities, and VA benefits generally don't pay for care outside the VA unless the VA authorizes it.

TRICARE For Life

You need both Medicare Part A and Part B to have it. Medicare pays first and TRICARE For Life pays second.

CHAMPVA

Once you're eligible for Medicare at 65, you need Part A and Part B to keep CHAMPVA. It then pays after Medicare.

The Part B decision

VA health care on its own doesn't protect you from the Part B late penalty. If you skip Part B at 65 and want it later, your premium goes up 10% for each full 12 months you waited, and you may have to wait for the General Enrollment Period to sign up.

Having Part B means you can see any doctor or hospital that takes Medicare, which matters if the nearest VA facility is a long drive or if VA funding or your priority group changes.

Prescriptions and Part D

VA drug coverage counts as creditable coverage. If you have it, you can go without a Part D plan and not owe the Part D late penalty if you join one later. Some veterans still add drug coverage so they can fill prescriptions from non-VA doctors at a local pharmacy.
